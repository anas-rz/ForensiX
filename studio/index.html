A deepfake detector for one person, built in your browser

Give it real photos, videos or voice recordings of one person. It learns their face or voice, then flags media that doesn't match them, without ever seeing a fake. It runs on your GPU or CPU, tests on your own real and fake files, and saves a small model you can use again here or in Python.

How it works

Faces: each frame's face is found (YuNet), aligned and described by a face-recognition network (SFace). Voice: each few seconds of speech is described by a speaker-recognition network (ECAPA-TDNN). Training learns the person's profile, the average of their descriptions, and how much their own media varies. A file's similarity is its median match to that profile; below the network's published threshold (0.363 for faces, 0.25 for voices) it's flagged.

Both networks are pretrained and run here unchanged: in our tests, training a small network on one person's media did worse than this. Expect it to catch face swaps and voice clones that don't fully reach the person's identity, and to miss ones that do; pair it with a deepfake detector. Real media recorded very differently (another camera, room or microphone) can also score lower, so train on varied real media and check the false-alarm rate on real files it hasn't seen.
