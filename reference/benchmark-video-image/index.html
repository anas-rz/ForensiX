    ForensiX benchmark: SDFVD (face-swap videos)¶

    Every ForensiX image and video detector was scored on the same 106 videos. Raw results are in results/sdfvd.json (metrics and per-video scores), results/sdfvd_scores.csv (one row per video) and results/sdfvd.log.

    Dataset¶

    • Name: SDFVD.
    • Link: https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/bcmkfgct2s/1
    • License: CC BY 4.0.
    • Size: 106 videos. 53 are real (SDFVD/videos_real/v1.mp4 … v53.mp4) and 53 are fake (SDFVD/videos_fake/vs1.mp4 … vs53.mp4). The archive is 182 MB, with sha256 734a101fdfd89d5b20ac69f510d6a7cf3b588a145c1993ba2488322ae50d95a5.
    • Real videos: short stock clips from Pexels, 2.1–5.8 s long (median 3.3 s), at 30 fps. Resolutions are 1280x720 (26), 1280x674 (11), 378x720 (8) and 404x720 (8).
    • Fake videos: a face swap of each real clip, made with Remaker AI. The dataset uses one face-swap tool for all fakes.
    • Format-matched pairs: each fake vsN comes from real vN and has the same resolution, frame rate, frame count and codec. So a detector can't separate the classes by file format, resolution or length; it has to respond to the manipulation itself.

    Setup¶

    • Hardware: NVIDIA L4 (23 GB, driver 580.82.07) on Google Colab Pro.
    • Date: 2026-10-26.
    • Code: ForensiX commit 16649a37fd31585fa49641c9f2a3ede17fbbe135. Scored with scripts/eval_video_folder.py as it is at that commit; no code changes were needed.
    • Software: Python 3.13.16, torch 2.11.0+cu130, OpenCV 5.0.0, numpy 2.1.3, timm 1.0.30, transformers 5.20.2.
    • Image detectors: each video gets 8 evenly spaced full frames. The frames are not face-cropped. Each frame goes through the model's own preprocessing, and the video score is the mean of the 8 frame scores. Most of these models score only a central crop of the frame without resizing it (univfd, rine: 224x224; safe: 256x256). On a 1280x720 frame, that crop covers only a small part of the image and may miss some or all of the face.
    • Video detectors: each uses its own upstream frame sampling and face pipeline.
    • gend, gend-pe, dfdet-clip: MTCNN, largest face in 32 frames, 5-point alignment.
    • dfdc-selim: MTCNN, all faces in 32 frames, 7 EfficientNet-B7 models.
    • dfdc-wm: RetinaFace, largest face in every 10th frame, up to 25 faces.
    • Threshold: every model's default threshold is 0.5 on fake_prob. Balanced, real and fake accuracy are measured at that threshold.
    • No-face fallback: these video models return exactly 0.5 when no face is found. No video got that score from any model, so every video model found a face in every video.

    Analysis¶

    • Only the three face-based CLIP/PE detectors work well here.
    • gend-pe, gend and dfdet-clip reach AUC 0.92–0.96 and EER 0.13–0.17, and they are the only models above 0.8 balanced accuracy at the default threshold.
    • All three were trained on FaceForensics++ with aligned face crops, which matches this task.
    • Their AUC differences are within noise on this dataset. Paired bootstrap 95% CIs: gend-pe − gend +0.037 [−0.008, +0.087], gend-pe − dfdet-clip +0.040 [−0.008, +0.092], gend − dfdet-clip +0.003 [−0.028, +0.035].
    • dfdet-clip beats the next model, dfdc-selim, by +0.154 AUC [+0.058, +0.258].
    • The two DFDC winners are weak here. dfdc-selim (AUC 0.761) and dfdc-wm (0.699) are face-based too, but they were trained on DFDC fakes, not on this tool's output. Neither is clearly better than the image detectors: for example, dfdc-selim − rine is +0.016 AUC [−0.092, +0.181].
    • Most models are miscalibrated at the default threshold. They rank fakes above reals better than chance, but nearly all their scores fall below 0.5, so almost everything is called real:
    • rine and safe score every video below 0.5. They get 100% of reals and 0% of fakes right, giving exactly 0.500 balanced accuracy despite AUC 0.745 and 0.693. Their highest fake scores are 0.384 and 0.276.
    • univfd (AUC 0.719) catches 1.9% of fakes; commfor-224 catches 3.8% and commfor 13.2%.
    • dfdc-selim catches 18.9% of fakes and dfdc-wm 26.4%, while getting 92–94% of reals right.
    • These models need a threshold chosen on in-domain data before their binary decisions are usable. AUC and EER are the fairer comparison for them.
    • gend and gend-pe lean the other way. They catch 94–96% of fakes but get only 68% and 76% of reals right, so they will raise false alarms on real faces at 0.5. dfdet-clip is more balanced at 0.5: it gets 89% of reals and 76% of fakes right.
    • clipdet is the best calibrated image detector at 0.641 balanced accuracy. Its AUC (0.712) is in line with the other image detectors.
    • The image detectors are expected to be weak here.
    • univfd, rine, rine-ldm, commfor, commfor-224, safe and clipdet were trained to flag whole images from GANs and diffusion models (ProGAN, Latent/Stable Diffusion and similar).
    • In a face swap, the background, body and most of the frame are untouched real camera footage, and only the face region is generated and blended in. That region is a small part of a 1280x720 frame.
    • Several of these models score only a central crop without resizing, so they may not see the face at all.
    • Their AUCs of 0.56–0.75 show some weak signal. All of their 95% CIs reach down to between 0.45 and 0.62, and rine-ldm's includes 0.5. They should not be used as face-swap detectors based on this run.

    Follow-up 1: image detectors on face crops¶

    The main run fed whole frames to the image detectors. As a follow-up, the same 8 frames per video were cropped around the largest face before scoring.

    • Option: --face-crop, added to scripts/eval_video_folder.py for this.
    • Crop: MTCNN, 1.3x the face box, at least 256 px a side, never resized, so each model's own preprocessing still applies.
    • Frames without a face: dropped. Every video kept at least one.
    • Results: results/sdfvd_facecrop.json, results/sdfvd_facecrop_scores.csv, results/sdfvd_facecrop.log.
    • Gain: the change in AUC from full frames to face crops on the same videos. Its 95% CI comes from 5,000 bootstrap resamples (reals and fakes drawn separately, seed 0), applied to both inputs.

    • Five of the seven models improve significantly on face crops; safe and clipdet don't change significantly. This supports the earlier explanation: on full frames, the center crop often missed the swapped face.
    • The best image detectors on crops (commfor, commfor-224, rine) are still below the face-based video detectors (gend-pe 0.956, gend 0.919, dfdet-clip 0.916).
    • The miscalibration remains. At 0.5, rine, univfd and safe still call almost every video real.
    • The rine row uses unrounded scores. The results files store per-video scores rounded to 6 decimals, and many of rine's scores round to 0 (36 of 106 full-frame scores, 20 of 106 face-crop scores). Recomputing its AUC from the stored scores therefore gives 0.719 instead of 0.745 for full frames. The AUCs in the JSON models entries were computed before rounding and are correct. The rine gain was computed from a rerun that kept full precision; it matched the stored scores after rounding.

    Follow-up 2: calibration¶

    forensix/calibration.py and scripts/recalibrate.py (see the README's Calibration section) fit, per model:

    • a Platt scaling of the score;
    • a split-conformal threshold on real videos only, so the false-alarm rate is at most α;
    • conformal prediction sets.

    Out-of-fold results. To estimate how calibration behaves on unseen videos, the scores in results/sdfvd_scores.csv were cross-validated:

    • 5 folds, seed 0, α = 5%.
    • Each real/fake pair (vN, vsN) is kept in the same fold, so a model is never calibrated on one half of a pair and tested on the other.
    • Results are in results/calibration_sdfvd.json.
    • The tables in calibrations/sdfvd/ are then fitted on all 106 videos.

    • Calibration changes the threshold, not the ranking. AUC is unchanged by construction.
    • Platt scaling fixes the "everything is real" problem. Models whose balanced accuracy at 0.5 was about 0.50 reach 0.63–0.70. ECE drops for every model except clipdet (0.052 → 0.114), which was already the best calibrated.
    • The conformal threshold held its false-alarm target out of fold. Observed rates were 3.8–7.5% at α = 5%. With about 10 reals per fold, each rate has wide error.
    • It catches few fakes for the weak models. The cost of a guaranteed false-alarm rate is low detection: 49–68% for the three GenD models, and at most 28% for the others.
    • Prediction sets are often {real, fake}. That means "this score doesn't support a decision at 95% confidence". The single-label column shows how often a model commits.
    • The tables were fitted on SDFVD. They encode Remaker AI swaps on Pexels clips. Refit them on data like yours before using them; scripts/recalibrate.py check tests whether a table still fits new labelled data.

    Caveats¶

    • Small sample. With 53 real and 53 fake videos, the 95% CI on AUC is about ±0.03–0.05 for the top models and about ±0.1 for models near AUC 0.7 (see the table). Differences of a few AUC points are not meaningful, and the ranking from dfdc-selim down to commfor is not reliable. Accuracy at a threshold has binomial error of up to about ±0.13 per class (95%, n=53).
    • One face-swap tool. All fakes come from Remaker AI on short Pexels clips. These results say nothing about other face-swap tools, reenactment, lip-sync, fully generated videos, or heavily compressed (social-media) footage.
    • Face detector substitution. Upstream GenD uses the SCRFD face detector. ForensiX uses MTCNN for gend, gend-pe and dfdet-clip (see forensix/video/gend/__init__.py), so their face crops can differ slightly from upstream.
    • Image detectors on video. The main table uses whole frames. Face crops change the numbers substantially; see Follow-up 1.
    • Timing. Times other than univfd and rine were measured with other models sharing the GPU (see the Results notes).
    • Run history. The run was split across processes to use the whole GPU:
    • univfd and rine come from a sequential run of the command below.
    • The other ten models come from parallel runs of the same script with --models subsets and their own --out files, merged into results/sdfvd.json.
    • Then the command below was rerun; it skipped all 12 models and wrote the CSV.
    • Splitting doesn't change scores. rine was scored twice, and rine-ldm, commfor, commfor-224 and safe were scored both alone and in parallel; per-video scores were identical every time. results/sdfvd.log has the full history.

    Reproduce¶

    # from the repo root, with ForensiX installed (pip install -e .) and a CUDA GPU
mkdir -p /content/sdfvd && cd /content/sdfvd
curl -L -o sdfvd.zip "https://data.mendeley.com/public-files/datasets/bcmkfgct2s/files/a9ef71a3-b625-4682-a21c-f0579e85cdd4/file_downloaded"
echo "734a101fdfd89d5b20ac69f510d6a7cf3b588a145c1993ba2488322ae50d95a5  sdfvd.zip" | sha256sum -c
unzip -q sdfvd.zip && cd -

export FORENSIX_CACHE=/path/to/forensix_cache   # optional; about 6 GB of weights
python scripts/eval_video_folder.py --device cuda \
  --real /content/sdfvd/SDFVD/videos_real --fake /content/sdfvd/SDFVD/videos_fake \
  --dataset SDFVD \
  --models univfd rine rine-ldm commfor commfor-224 safe clipdet gend gend-pe dfdet-clip dfdc-wm dfdc-selim \
  --out results/sdfvd.json --scores-out results/sdfvd_scores.csv

    Follow-ups (same downloads):

    # 1. image detectors on face crops
python scripts/eval_video_folder.py --device cuda --face-crop \
  --real /content/sdfvd/SDFVD/videos_real --fake /content/sdfvd/SDFVD/videos_fake --dataset SDFVD \
  --models univfd rine rine-ldm commfor commfor-224 safe clipdet \
  --out results/sdfvd_facecrop.json --scores-out results/sdfvd_facecrop_scores.csv
# 2. calibration: cross-validated report and one table per model, from the stored scores
python scripts/recalibrate.py fit --scores results/sdfvd_scores.csv --group-regex '(\d+)' \
  --alpha 0.05 --out-dir calibrations/sdfvd --report results/calibration_sdfvd.json

    The script resumes: models already in --out are skipped. To use more of a large GPU, run several copies with disjoint --models lists and separate --out files, then merge their models and scores entries. The metrics are unchanged.
