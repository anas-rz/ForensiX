    Interpreting scores¶

    A detector's output is evidence, not a verdict. This guide explains what each part of a Prediction means, and how to turn scores into decisions you can defend.

    The three kinds of score¶

    score_type threshold Models Meaning
    "probability" a number (usually 0.5) audio, image, video, stablesyncnet, avcons, avid, person-detector The model's softmax or sigmoid output. In [0, 1], but not calibrated: 0.8 doesn't mean "80% chance of fake".
    "anomaly" None avad, syncnet, phys-*, physdf Distance from how real media behaves. There's no natural cut-off: choose one on your own real data. syncnet's score is -confidence, so it's usually negative.
    "anomaly" a number id-face (0.637), id-motion 1 − similarity to the enrolled person. The threshold comes from face verification (LFW) or training, not from deepfake data.

    In every case, higher means more likely fake, so ranking metrics (AUC, EER) work the same way for all of them.

    Why the default threshold often fails¶

    A model's default threshold of 0.5 fits the data it was trained on. On other data, the score distributions shift. On the SDFVD face-swap benchmark:

    Model AUC Real correct at 0.5 Fake correct at 0.5 What happened
    rine 0.745 100% 0% Every score below 0.5; highest fake 0.384
    dfdc-selim 0.761 94% 19% Scores too low on this face-swap tool
    gend 0.919 68% 94% Scores too high on real faces
    dfdet-clip 0.916 89% 76% Reasonably balanced

    A model can rank well (good AUC) and still make bad yes/no decisions at its default threshold. Treat the default as a placeholder.

    Choosing a threshold¶

    1. Collect real media like the media you'll check: same sources, platforms, compression, resolution, languages. Real data is much easier to get than fakes, and it's what sets the false-alarm rate.
    2. Decide the false-alarm rate you can afford: for example 1% of real uploads sent to human review.
    3. Set the threshold at that quantile of the real scores (recipe 5).
    4. Measure the detection rate on fakes, if you have any, at that threshold. Measure it on media not used to set the threshold.
    5. Re-check periodically. New cameras, codecs and platforms shift real-media scores too.

    Sample size matters. To confirm a 1% false-alarm rate to within about ±0.5 points, you need roughly 1,500 real samples; to within ±0.2 points, about 10,000.

    Segments and localization¶

    segment_fake_probs breaks a score down:

    Models One segment per Use
    audio (most) 4.04 s window (recipe 3) Find the fake part of a long recording
    nes2net-x-itw 30 s chunk Coarse localization
    video models scored face crop Spot one bad face among good ones; empty means no face found
    avcons AV window When audio and face stop matching
    id-face sampled frame (2 per second) When the face stops being the person
    id-motion 4 s window Same, for behaviour
    person-detector (video) frame Same, for the per-person model

    Physics detectors put their timelines in details["timeline"], and physdf puts segments in details["localization"]["segments"], each with a start and end in seconds, the constraint and the face or voice node involved.

    Clip-level scores average over segments, so a short manipulation in a long clip can be diluted below the threshold. If short edits matter, also flag on max(segment_fake_probs), with its own threshold chosen the same way.

    details¶

    details holds structured evidence, and its keys depend on the model (API reference). The most useful ones:

    • syncnet: offset_frames (best audio shift; real clips are usually within ±3 frames, and an offset at ±15 means no good alignment was found), confidence (about 10 on a clean real clip, under 1 when audio and lips don't match), mean_dist_by_offset (the full curve).
    • id-face: similarity, frame_similarity, frame_time_s, frames_without_face, and other_enrolled_similarity, which tells you when the face looks more like someone else in your gallery.
    • Physics detectors: constraints[name] with measured, expected_range_start, worse (whether higher or lower is suspicious), violation (0–1) and prior (provisional or learned).
    • physdf: report (verdict, findings, counter-evidence, and Markdown), localization, status (which checks ran).

    Reporting results to people¶

    • Report the score with the model name and threshold, never a bare "87% fake".
    • Say what the model checks: "the face region shows manipulation artefacts" (gend-pe) is a different claim from "the voice doesn't match the lip movements" (syncnet).
    • A low score is not proof of authenticity. It means this model found nothing. Models miss manipulations they weren't trained for.
    • Keep a human in the loop for consequential decisions. The stablesyncnet weights license forbids fully automated decisions that affect someone's legal rights (Licensing).
