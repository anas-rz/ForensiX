    Identity and person models¶

    Most detectors judge media on its own. Identity-based detectors also know who the media claims to show, and compare it with reference media of that person. This fits person-of-interest protection: executives, public figures, or customers who enrolled for verification. It needs nothing about how a fake was made.

    ForensiX has three tools for this:

    Tool Question Enroll from Needs training?
    id-face Does the face look like the person? Photos and/or videos No (SFace weights)
    id-motion Does the person move and emote like the person? Videos only Yes, once, on many people (Colab)
    person-detector Is this real media of this one person? Their real media (any modality) Yes, per person, seconds on CPU

    What each one catches¶

    Attack id-face id-motion person-detector
    A different person claiming to be X Yes Yes Yes
    Poor face swap of X Often Yes Often
    Good face swap of X, with an actor's motion No: built to look like X Yes: the motion is the actor's Depends on the training data
    Reenactment of X driven by someone else No Yes Depends
    Real X in unusual conditions (lighting, age) May false-alarm May false-alarm May false-alarm

    A good face swap is designed to pass face recognition. Use id-face together with a face-manipulation detector (gend-pe, dfdet-clip), and with id-motion where you have videos of the person.

    id-face¶

    import forensix

det = forensix.load("id-face")                        # CPU is fine
det.enroll("alice", ["alice_passport.jpg", "alice_interview.mp4"])
det.save_gallery("people.npz")

pred = det.predict("new_video.mp4", identity="alice")
pred.is_fake                                  # True when similarity < 0.363 (fake_prob > 0.637)
pred.details["similarity"]                    # median over frames of the best-matching face
pred.details["frame_similarity"]              # per sampled frame (2 per second, up to 120 frames)
pred.details["frames_without_face"]
pred.details["other_enrolled_similarity"]     # {"bob": 0.72} means it looks more like bob

det.identify("unknown.jpg")                   # [("alice", 0.61), ("bob", 0.12)]

    How it works: - Detection and embedding: YuNet finds faces and 5 landmarks, OpenCV aligns each face to 112×112, and SFace embeds it. The 0.363 cosine threshold is OpenCV Zoo's, which gives 99.40% on LFW. - Enrollment: each frame contributes its largest face. In videos, frames whose face disagrees with the rest (another person, a bad detection) are dropped. - Verification: in each frame, the face that looks most like the person is kept (the mean of its top-3 similarities to the references). The clip score is the median over frames, which resists a few bad frames, while the per-frame timeline shows a face switching mid-video.

    Enrollment tips: - Use 5–20 references covering different angles, lighting, ages and expressions. One passport photo works but is brittle. - Enroll from video when you can: it gives many frames, filtered for consistency. - Re-enroll as people age or change appearance. - Galleries hold embeddings, not images, but they are still biometric data. Store and delete them under the rules that apply to you (GDPR, BIPA and similar).

    id-motion¶

    det = forensix.load("id-motion", weights_path="id_motion.safetensors")   # train it first
det.enroll("alice", ["alice_interview1.mp4", "alice_interview2.mp4"])
pred = det.predict("new.mp4", identity="alice")
pred.details["window_similarity"], pred.details["window_start_s"]       # per 4 s window
    • Features: head rotation, translation and their velocities, plus MediaPipe's 52 expression blendshapes, at 25 fps. These describe behaviour, not the face's shape.
    • Model: a temporal network embeds every 4 s window. It is trained contrastively on real videos of many people, with each person's positives taken from different videos, so it learns the person rather than the recording.
    • Data needs: a few seconds of natural, talking behaviour in both the references and the query. Several reference videos in different settings work best. Short clips, or a person who barely moves, give weak evidence.
    • Training: on VoxCeleb2 dev on Colab (Training). The decision threshold is the equal-error point measured on held-out people.

    person-detector¶

    A tiny model (~23k parameters) trained for one person, on CPU, in seconds. Without negatives, it synthesizes manipulated and impostor-like samples from the real ones.

    from forensix.specialized import train_person_model

det = train_person_model("alice", real_media=alice_files, negative_media=other_people_files,
                         modality="video", epochs=30)
det.save("alice.safetensors")
forensix.load("person-detector", weights_path="alice.safetensors").predict("new.mp4")
    • modality is image, audio, video, audiovisual or auto; one model covers one modality.
    • use_peft=True (default) adapts a base model with LoRA. pretrain_base_model builds that base from general real/fake data; without it, a fresh base is used.
    • Real negatives (other people, known fakes of the person) beat synthetic ones. Add them when you have them.
    • Hold out some of the person's real media and measure the false-alarm rate on it before relying on the model. A few training samples give an overconfident model.

    Evaluating identity detectors¶

    scripts/eval_identity.py enrolls each person from references/<person>/, then scores their real queries (should pass), their fakes (should be flagged) and other people's real queries claimed as them (impostors):

    DATA/references/<person>/*        reference photos or videos
DATA/queries/<person>/real/*      genuine media of the person
DATA/queries/<person>/fake/*      deepfakes of the person (optional)

    python scripts/eval_identity.py --data /content/id_eval --models id-face id-motion \
    --weights id-motion=id_motion.safetensors --out results/identity.json

    There's no commercially usable deepfake benchmark with per-person references. VoxCeleb2 test (CC BY 4.0) alone already measures the impostor rate and the false-alarm rate on real media.
