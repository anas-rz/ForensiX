    Fairness reports¶

    A detector that flags real people as fake more often when they have darker skin, are older, or are women causes real harm, and buyers and regulators (the EU AI Act, NIST's face-analysis guidance, procurement questionnaires) ask for evidence that it doesn't. A ForensiX fairness report measures false-alarm rates by skin tone, age and gender (and any other attribute you label) for any image, video or identity model, at the threshold the model actually runs at.

    What it measures¶

    For each model and each attribute (for example gender), the report gives, per group:

    Measure Meaning
    False-alarm rate Share of the group's real items the model flags as fake, with a 95% Wilson interval. The main number
    Detection rate Share of the group's fake items caught, when the dataset has fakes
    AUC Per-group ranking quality, when the group has both real and fake items
    Reliable Whether the group has at least min_group real items (default 30)

    and a disparity across the reliable groups: the highest and lowest false-alarm rates, their difference and ratio, and a two-sided Fisher exact test of whether the gap could be chance (significant at p < 0.05). Groups below min_group are still shown, marked ⚠, but left out of the comparison: 1 false alarm in 8 people is not evidence of anything.

    The threshold matters. Rates are measured at the model's operating point: its active calibration's threshold ("calibration vN"), else the server's configured threshold ("server config"), else the model default. The report records which was used. Re-run it after you recalibrate.

    Provenance models (c2pa, watermarks, metadata) read file evidence, not faces, and are rejected.

    The dataset¶

    Any ForensiX dataset with demographic columns in its manifest:

    file,label,group,skin_tone,age,gender,attr_region
media/p001.jpg,real,p001,4,34,female,south-asia
media/p002.jpg,real,p002,9,61,male,west-africa
media/p002_swap.jpg,fake,p002,9,61,male,west-africa
    Column Values Grouped as
    skin_tone Monk 1-10 or Fitzpatrick I-VI Monk 1-3 / 4-7 / 8-10; Fitzpatrick I-II / III-IV / V-VI
    age years, or a band such as 20-29 0-17, 18-29, 30-44, 45-59, 60+ (bands are kept as given)
    gender any label as given, lower-cased
    attr_<name> any label as given, reported as <name>

    Real items drive the false-alarm rates, so a fairness set can be all real. Aim for at least 30, better 100+, real items per group; with 30 the interval on a 5% rate is roughly 1–17%.

    No skin-tone labels? Estimate them¶

    With estimate_skin_tone on (the default) and no skin_tone column, ForensiX estimates each face's Individual Typology Angle (ITA, from the CIELAB colour of the cheeks and forehead, found with the YuNet face detector) and groups the dataset into terciles: lighter, middle and darker third. Faces that are greyscale, in profile or too small are left out.

    Treat this as a proxy, not a measurement. Lighting, white balance and make-up move ITA a lot, so the tercile labels are relative to this dataset, never absolute skin types. Use annotated Monk labels for anything you publish. On FairFace, faces annotated Black fall in the darker third 55% of the time and in the lighter third 12%; that's useful for screening but noisy.

    A ready audit set: FairFace¶

    FairFace (CC BY 4.0) has real face photos annotated with age band, gender and race. scripts/build_fairface_set.py builds a balanced audit set (default 30 per race × gender cell, 420 images) from its validation split; the script's docstring has the download command. Race is FairFace's annotation and is reported as race. It is not skin tone.

    Running a report¶

    Web manager: open Fairness, pick the dataset and models, and start. The report shows, per model and attribute, each group's false-alarm rate and interval against the overall rate, with disparity verdicts, and downloads as Markdown or JSON.

    HTTP:

    curl -X POST localhost:8000/v1/fairness -H "X-API-Key: $ADMIN" -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{"dataset": "19065db78962", "models": ["commfor", "univfd"], "estimate_skin_tone": true, "min_group": 30}'
# → 202 {"id": "f6f17dfe2b81", ...}; follow GET /v1/jobs/{id}
curl localhost:8000/v1/fairness/f6f17dfe2b81?format=markdown -H "X-API-Key: $KEY" > fairness.md
    Field Default Meaning
    dataset or datasets One dataset id, or several pooled
    models Image, video or identity models
    estimate_skin_tone true Estimate ITA terciles when there's no skin_tone column
    min_group 30 Real items a group needs to enter the disparity comparison
    attributes skin_tone, age, gender, race Which attributes to report

    GET /v1/fairness lists saved reports. They are kept in the calibration directory with the datasets they came from.

    Python:

    from forensix.fairness import report, to_markdown

items = [{"label": "real", "attributes": {"gender": "female", "age": "34"}, "scores": {"commfor": 0.12}}, ...]
rep = report(items, {"commfor": {"threshold": 0.5, "threshold_source": "model default"}})
print(to_markdown(rep))

    Reading a report honestly¶

    • A significant disparity is a finding: say which groups, by how much, at which threshold. Not significant means the data can't show a gap, not that there is none. Check how wide the intervals are.
    • False alarms depend on the images as much as the people: camera, compression and lighting differ between sources, so collect every group from comparable sources.
    • Recalibrating moves the threshold for everyone. Lowering one group's false alarms with a per-group threshold is a policy decision with legal implications; ForensiX doesn't do it for you.
